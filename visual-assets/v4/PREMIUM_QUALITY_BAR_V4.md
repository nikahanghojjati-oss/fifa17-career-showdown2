# PREMIUM QUALITY BAR V4

Owner baseline:
Previously approved Home / League / Club visuals are approximately 6/10 for premiumness and must not be treated as the finish line.

V4 target:
Every major Level-A screen should feel intentionally art-directed, production-ready and unmistakably part of Career Mode Showdown.

## Ten hard visual dimensions

### Q1 — Product currency
The composition reflects the current product, not an old screenshot or stale flow.

### Q2 — Unified typography
One documented type system across the product:
- display
- functional UI
- body/support
- personality accent
No arbitrary per-screen font invention.

### Q3 — Cinematic environment
The football/stadium world is visibly present where appropriate.
Lighting, depth, atmosphere and camera framing support the screen story.
The environment cannot be technically present but visually erased.

### Q4 — Screen-specific concept
Each major screen has one memorable visual idea:
Home = rivalry headquarters
League = selection ceremony
Club = sealed reveal
Transfer = deadline war room
Results = judgment / confrontation
Summary = resolution / celebration
A generic panel grid fails.

### Q5 — Composition and hierarchy
The eye knows what matters within two seconds.
Primary action, dominant object, character art and secondary information do not compete.

### Q6 — Material craft
Panels feel authored:
- layered graphite
- controlled gold
- edge treatment
- texture
- light
- inset depth
- meaningful clipping
Avoid "border + background + border-radius = component."

### Q7 — Spatial rhythm
Use a controlled spacing/grid system.
Alignment, margins, gutters, baseline rhythm and component density feel intentional.
Random padding values are a failure.

### Q8 — Motion and interaction
Motion communicates state, feedback or emphasis.
It is brief, purposeful, cancelable/optional where appropriate, and reduced-motion-safe.
No decorative motion that delays play.

### Q9 — Responsive recomposition
Desktop, Chromebook/tablet and mobile are deliberately composed for their context.
Mobile is not a miniature desktop.
Important content and actions remain obvious.

### Q10 — Craft / polish
No visibly accidental details:
- inconsistent border weights
- type drift
- mismatched shadows
- awkward crop
- dead zones
- low-quality asset edges
- weak hover/focus states
- conflicting radii
- unbalanced optical spacing
- placeholder-feeling UI

## Premium rejection triggers

Reject before owner review if any are true:
- "AI/vibe-coded" first impression
- generic SaaS dashboard appearance
- same cards repeated everywhere
- black background with gold borders but no spatial/world depth
- inconsistent title typography
- stadium/background effectively invisible
- character art feels pasted on rather than lit into the scene
- large unused dead zones
- decorative complexity without hierarchy
- current product behavior missing from the visual proposal

## Quality principle from research

Premium does not mean maximal decoration.

Apple's design guidance emphasizes purpose, hierarchy, craft and delight.
Figma emphasizes connected typography, elevation, layout, grids and spacing.
Webflow's advanced visual-developer path emphasizes scalable systems, responsive behavior, interactions and quality review.
Awwwards' design curriculum repeatedly centers art direction, typography and purposeful motion.

V4 therefore treats polish as systemic precision, not ornament count.
