## Intent

The owner reference establishes the presentation target for Transfer War: a premium, cinematic football operations desk in which the managers, physical environment, task surfaces and information feel like one designed world.

The reference is a **visual north star**, not a literal asset or layout to copy. Do not reproduce proprietary game assets, player photos, logos, exact typography, names, or ornamental details from the reference.

## Core direction

The current visual system has drifted too far toward isolated rectangular cards and basic dashboard boxes. That is not the desired final language.

The target is:
- integrated rather than card-stacked;
- dimensional rather than flat;
- premium sports presentation rather than SaaS dashboard;
- dense with controlled detail, but with one obvious task hierarchy;
- physically grounded in the room/table rather than floating independently;
- asymmetrical and cinematic rather than a grid of equal panels.

## Anti-box rule

Cards may still exist as semantic/layout primitives, but they must not become the dominant visual language.

For Tier S Transfer screens:
- avoid walls of equal rectangular cards;
- avoid large opaque boxes floating over the scene with no physical relationship to it;
- avoid repeated identical borders, radii and shadows that read as a web dashboard;
- use one primary task substrate at most, visually integrated with the desk/glass/display system;
- secondary information should prefer slim plates, embedded rails, edge-lit glass, dossiers, bands, chips, table projections, nameplates or physically motivated overlays;
- borders can be partial, chamfered, layered, interrupted or material-driven rather than complete rectangles everywhere.

If a live form needs a strong contrast surface for accessibility, increase local tint/scrim directly behind the live text and controls. Do not solve readability by turning the whole composition into opaque cards.

## Premium material language

Preferred language:
- smoked / dark glass with controlled translucency;
- black graphite and near-black metal structure;
- brass / warm gold edge light as a priority accent, not a blanket tint;
- reflections, contact shadows and layered occlusion;
- luminous seams, thin technical dividers and local glow where physically motivated;
- stadium light and practical light spilling into the workspace;
- table-integrated tactical markings and restrained micro-detail;
- surface depth created by perspective, overlap and contact, not by decorative drop shadows alone.

Transparency is adjustable. Readability and contrast win, but opacity should be localized so the premium glass/desk language survives.

## Manager staging

Daniel and Nik must feel like people actively inhabiting the same transfer room, not portraits pasted beside UI.

The frame should make it obvious:
- what each manager is looking at;
- what each manager is physically doing;
- where the task surface sits relative to them;
- how the table/display/props connect their actions;
- where the key light and environmental light come from.

Their poses can flank or partially overlap task surfaces when product truth allows. Faces remain protected and readable.

## Guess Entry / rival privacy treatment

The rival's private guesses remain unavailable by product truth. Premium presentation must not fake data or progress.

Do not represent the hidden rival state as a generic blank card.

Preferred metaphors include:
- a sealed physical dossier integrated into the desk;
- a frosted / smoked glass bay with a lock/seal;
- three identical unrevealed plates recessed into the table/display;
- an inactive illuminated compartment or sealed tactical folder.

It must still reveal **zero** rival guesses, counts, progress or lock state beyond what production knows.

## Live task surface

The viewer's own Guess Entry remains semantic DOM and readable.

It should feel like an active scouting / transfer workstation:
- one strong working surface;
- compact integrated input rows;
- strong primary LOCK action;
- hierarchy from light, material and placement rather than a giant surrounding rectangle;
- task controls visually belong to the desk/display system.

## Composition target

At desktop key frames, aim for:
- clear foreground / working surface;
- managers in the middle plane;
- stadium / room depth behind;
- UI embedded across those planes without sacrificing semantic DOM;
- one dominant action area and several restrained supporting details;
- premium density without functional clutter.

On mobile, recomposition is allowed. Do not merely stack every desktop box vertically. Preserve manager identity and one premium material cue while simplifying secondary decoration.
