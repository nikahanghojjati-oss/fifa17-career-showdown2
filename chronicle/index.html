The Chronicle, Chapter One

Somewhere between a console and a phone, a rivalry needed a referee. Nik and Daniel were each deep in a FIFA 17 career of their own: two saves, two dressing rooms, and one question that mattered between them. Who had done more with their club?

The answer could not live in a group chat. It needed wheels to choose the league and the club, a fair way to count trophies, and a memory that would never lose a season. On 30 July 2026 the first line of code was committed to answer it. Nobody guessed how far that line would run.

What followed was built by a studio of models and judged by one owner. Claude, GPT and Codex wrote the code, argued in review, broke things and mended them. Nik and Daniel played it on their own phones and reported what failed. The rules hardened as they went: fifteen points a season at most, a hundred and fifty for a Golden Showdown, and never a tiebreak, because equal is a draw.

Today a Showdown begins when Daniel chooses its length: one, three, five or ten seasons. The roulette spins, the Prime Pack opens, the Transfer War clock starts its fifteen minutes, and Marco Reus waits on the loading screen in black and gold. On 2 November, version 2.0.0 went live. Version 2.1.0 is loading the Chamber Men.

This is the whole story, told as a book.

01 / THE ORIGIN

It began with
a game between mates.

A free place to keep score. A few wheels to decide the league and clubs. The nostalgic feeling of FIFA 17.

The recovered owner decision index records the first brief: a Career Mode Showdown tracker for a competition against a mate. Each manager would play their own FIFA save; the website would organize the rivalry around it. Football matches themselves would stay outside the companion.

The essentials became the project's identity: just two managers, the same league, two different permanent clubs, trophy-based scoring and a timed Transfer War. Daniel is Manager 1, on the left. Nik is Manager 2, on the right. Even that visual order became an explicit design rule.

THE CAUSAL HISTORY / LESSONS THAT BECAME STRUCTURE

The idea stayed small.
The foundations grew deeper.

Some of the most consequential decisions were corrections from the owner.

The historical review records that an early design allowed clubs or leagues to vary between seasons. Nik corrected it: one league, two distinct clubs, assigned once and kept for the whole Showdown. Clubs could be reused in a later Showdown. The rule gave the rivalry continuity instead of treating every season as an unrelated draw.

The same happened with scoring. Older summaries had allowed both achievements in a bonus pair to score separately. The owner clarified the shared caps. The compact 5 / 3 / 1 trophy system and two single-point bonuses became an invariant that later pure protocols and ten-season sweeps could verify.

Transfer War anticipated future privacy before networking existed. Guess Entry and Signing Entry became separate phases; searchable league and nationality selectors replaced fragile free text. The historical account explicitly connects that separation to eventual two-device play.

The Showdown hub was also an intentional choice. Returning to the clubs, score and current season gave the rivalry an identity between rounds. The later scene work deepens that original purpose: a ceremony around the game, rather than a collection of forms.

THE RULES, MADE CONCRETE

Every point
has a reason.

The website computes the score. Both bonus pairs cap at one point each. Trophies and bonuses give up to 11 points a season. In a Showdown confirmed after the 2.0.0 release time, Prime Pack adds up to 4 more, so a season is worth up to 15 points and a ten-season Golden Showdown 150.

There is no tiebreak anywhere. More Showdown points wins a season; equal points is a draw. The final Showdown uses total Showdown points only: equal totals are a draw.

04 / THE WAY IT GETS BUILT

The project learned
how to manage itself.

The work moved from repeated chat instructions to repository-owned jobs, handbooks, status files, contracts and generated boards. Progress became durable enough to survive a new chat or an interrupted session.

POS20 / THE PROJECT OPERATING SYSTEM

A reasoning loop.
A preserved safety kernel.

POS20 adds evidence debt, ranked next actions and a rule to reframe after two failed corrections under unchanged evidence. Its predecessor, POS10, remains the minimum test-routing and heavy-proof kernel. POS20 may increase proof; it may not reduce that floor.

Recovery is expressed as READY, STALE or BLOCKED, with an objective, blocker and next verification. Process work itself earns no product-readiness credit.

THE FACTORY / SCALE, DELIVERY & LEARNING

The impressive part:
the work became observable.

A visual factory starts with 111 planned jobs. Its reviewed branch now contains 253 status files. The gameplay factory has its own 43 status records, integration path and QA pipeline. Splitting work changes the unit of counting; it also makes ownership, blockers and repair history visible.

The scale is process scale. A job may be a truth sheet, art preparation, desktop build, phone composition, review, repair, motion pass or integration task. These counts describe recorded work units—not hundreds of distinct shipped features.

The learning is visible in the record. The factory report studies which tasks needed fixes, how job size affected delivery, and when a different worker was needed. That makes the factory more than a queue: it can change how the next piece of work is assigned.

05 / A SMARTER CHECK SYSTEM

Showdown Gate.
Physio on the touchline.

REVIEWED STATE: SHADOW MODE

One selector waiting for a GitHub runner could hold up every dependent lane. The new Gate lets six lanes calculate their own route. Its seal recomputes the route and requires the selected evidence on the same commit.

At the reviewed main commit, the coverage map accounts for 63 steps across 16 existing gates. Showdown Gate runs alongside POS20. POS20 remains the merge authority while the replacement proves itself.

✚

SHOWDOWN GATE PHYSIO

Recover the machine. Preserve the failure.

Physio distinguishes infrastructure trouble from a test that actually started and failed. It allows at most two automatic retries per head for eligible infrastructure failures, leaves real test failures intact and ignores superseded heads. If checks wait for a machine, it can pause only allowlisted helpers, with a guard against interrupting a push.

03Visual acceptance is measured independently.+

The visual scorecard evaluates ten criteria: composition, characters, hands, lighting, typography, panels, clarity, motion, phone composition and finish. It also defines hard gates for truthful data, rights, accessibility, performance and mockup fidelity.

The static review target is an average of at least 4.2; motion/final review uses at least 4.4, with additional minimums. These are written acceptance targets, not claims that every current screen already passes.
