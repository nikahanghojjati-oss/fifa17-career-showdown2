## Alavés

Production name: `Alavés`

Facts: Deportivo Alavés' centenary history traces the current name to January 1921 after the Sport Friend's precursor and roots the club in Vitoria-Gasteiz/Álava. The club's own current site uses the `albiazul` identity language.

Sources:
- https://centenario.alaves.com/historia/
- https://deportivoalaves.com/club/historia-y-palmares
- https://memorias.laliga.es/2016-2017/clubes-laliga-santander/deportivo-alaves-sad.html

Safe design inputs: early-20th-century Basque/Álava local-club lineage; blue/white field language; restrained highland/inland geometry rather than a literal local emblem.

Exclude: copied official pennant/banner construction; official DA lettering; civic heraldry; visible 1921 dependency as the main recognition device.

## Athletic Club

Production name: `Athletic Club`

Facts: Athletic's official history dates foundation to 1898 and describes football reaching Bilbao through the River Nervión during rapid industrial development, introduced by British workers and Basques who had studied in Britain. The club initially used blue/white before adopting red/white in 1910.

Sources:
- https://www.athletic-club.eus/en/club/history/
- https://www.athletic-club.eus/en/news/2021/01/09/how-athletic-turned-red-and-white/

Safe design inputs: late-Victorian Bilbao; river/port/industrial arrival of football; red/white stripe language; old-club restrained geometry.

Exclude: copied Bilbao/San Mamés imagery; official shield field arrangement; copied AC monogram; municipal heraldry.

Exclude: bear/tree; copied official shield outline; Madrid civic heraldry; copied stars/stripe count/layout; official wordmark.

Sources:

Exclude: copied official cross/ball/letter arrangement; municipal arms; copied shield contour or SEF wording.

## Leganés

It records early blue/red, later green/white and finally local blue/white kit identity. The official history also explains the `pepineros` local nickname through the area's historical cucumber farming.

Sources:
- https://www.cdleganes.com/en/club/history

Exclude: laurel wreath copied from official crest; crown; literal cucumber mascot/device; official CD lettering.

## Osasuna

Production name: `Osasuna`

Facts: Osasuna's official history dates the club to 24 October 1920 in Pamplona after members of La Sportiva and a breakaway New Club reunited. The club says `Osasuna` means health in Basque and records the choice of red as a tribute to the flag of Navarre.

Source:
- https://www.osasuna.es/en/history1

Safe design inputs: reunification/fusion geometry; Pamplona/Navarre regional grounding; red/navy with gold accent; two forms converging into one compact identity.

Exclude: crown; lion; copied Navarre chains/heraldry; copied official shield; official CAO lettering.

## Ambiguity policy proven by this league

Several club histories do not reduce cleanly to one simple number or uninterrupted institutional line.

Examples include:

- Sevilla: 1890 lineage/activity and 1905 formal registration;
- Granada: multiple April 1931 milestones appear in official club historical material;
- Espanyol: 1900 foundation plus later cessation/reformation path;
- Málaga: football lineage from 1904 across multiple entities, with current Málaga CF name/entity from 1994;
- Sporting Gijón: accepted 1905 foundation with later formal reconstruction of the historical record.

Therefore founding year is an authoring signal, not a required rendered badge element. V2.1 preserves uncertainty rather than hiding it.
