# Club Identity V2.1 — Pilot History / Place Research Ledger

Status: PILOT AUTHORING RESEARCH / NOT RUNTIME DATA / NOT FINAL BADGE ART

Date: 2026-09-10

Purpose: prove the V2.1 research-to-original-design method on a varied sample before scaling the same method across all 98 supported clubs.

This file is proposal authoring evidence only. It must not be imported by production JavaScript and must not be written to Firebase, Firestore, Storage, Auth, Shared Journey, save data or provider payloads.

## Method

For each pilot club:

1. establish a small number of factual history/place cues from an official club source where available;
2. translate those facts into broad design-language inputs;
3. explicitly exclude distinctive official crest devices or civic heraldry;
4. pass only compact abstract descriptor choices to the future runtime identity table.

The goal is recognizable club character through factual context and broad football design language, not a modified copy of the official badge.

## Arsenal

Canonical production name: `Arsenal`

Verified factual cues:

- Arsenal's official site says the club was formed in 1886 as Dial Square by workers connected with the Woolwich armaments/munitions works.
- The club later moved from Woolwich to Highbury/Islington in north London in 1913.
- Arsenal's official historical material describes the early red-shirt connection through Nottingham Forest players.

Official sources:

- https://www.arsenal.com/feature/where-are-arsenal-located-in-london-a78bI0A8K1JA
- https://www.arsenal.com/history/ggm-16-royal-arsenal-formed-in-woolwich
- https://careers.arsenal.com/pages/about-arsenal

Authoring interpretation:

- founding era: late 19th century;
- origin type: industrial / works community;
- place context: Woolwich origin + later north-London/Islington identity;
- safe abstract cue: engineered panel/rivet/grid rhythm combined with a refined metropolitan shield treatment;
- historical color language: red-dominant with light neutral support.

Near-copy exclusions:

- no cannon;
- no copied Arsenal shield contour;
- no official wordmark/lettering arrangement;
- no near-copy of current or historic official crest layout.

## Crystal Palace — ambiguity handling example

Canonical production name: `Crystal Palace`

Verified factual cues:

- Crystal Palace's current official history claims an established link to a Crystal Palace football team formed in 1861.
- The same official history states the club became professional in 1905.

Official sources:

- https://www.cpfc.co.uk/information/crystal-palace-history/
- https://www.cpfc.co.uk/information/crystal-palace-1861-history/

Authoring interpretation:

- do not force one disputed/lineage-sensitive year into visible badge text;
- authoring can use a broad `heritage-victorian-to-early-professional` era classification;
- place context: south London / Crystal Palace area;
- safe abstract cue: faceted/glass-grid or exhibition-era geometry may be explored only as generic abstraction, not a literal Crystal Palace building silhouette;
- historical color language: red + blue.

Near-copy exclusions:

- no eagle;
- no literal Crystal Palace building;
- no copied official shield/crest geometry;
- no visible `1861` or `1905` in the badge unless a later product/legal/content decision explicitly authorizes date display.

This club demonstrates why V2.1 should separate factual research from runtime descriptors and why founding-year ambiguity should not create a product-state dependency.

## Pilot conclusion

The pilot supports the V2.1 two-layer architecture.

History/place research adds meaningful differentiation without requiring official crest imitation.

The strongest recurring safe inputs are:

- founding era;
- origin type such as works/railway/church/school/civic formation;
- broad city/region character;
- broad historical kit field language;
- club-associated palette.

The strongest safety pattern is to convert those facts into generic geometric categories and then explicitly ban the club's distinctive official emblem devices.

Next research scale-up should proceed league-by-league and record factual ambiguity rather than silently normalizing it.

No production badge generation should begin until the research/descriptor gate for the intended batch is complete.
