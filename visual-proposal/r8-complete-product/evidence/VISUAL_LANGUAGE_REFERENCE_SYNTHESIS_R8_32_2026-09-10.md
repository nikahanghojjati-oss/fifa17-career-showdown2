## Visual grammar confirmed by the reference family

1. **Three-plane composition** — atmospheric stadium at the back, Daniel/Nik as non-authoritative rivalry framing in the middle, live product UI in front.
2. **Persistent CM17 shell** — near-black top navigation with gold active state, compact CM17 identity, contextual secondary navigation where a real product hierarchy exists, and a restrained footer identity.
3. **Page-specific hero composition** — each page receives its own dominant object or information surface: wheel, rivalry table, timer, season score, trophy, checklist, form or pack theatre. Do not force every screen into one universal card grid.
4. **Near-black + warm-gold hierarchy** — charcoal/black carries visual mass; gold is an edge, state, light and CTA accent; white carries most functional text. Avoid the production gray-panel look as the proposal destination.
5. **Typography roles** — brush/display treatment for page identity, condensed uppercase for functional information, script only for personality labels and short slogans. Critical controls remain highly legible functional text.
6. **Decisive action hierarchy** — one visually obvious primary action, quieter secondary/back actions, explicit disabled/locked states.
7. **Desktop cinematic / mobile recomposition** — wide desktop may use full rivalry staging. Tablet and mobile may remove characters, reduce secondary atmosphere and stack live controls rather than shrinking the desktop tableau.

## Character expression / pose family

Target roughly six reusable, identity-consistent families per manager rather than generating a new identity per screen:

1. hero / welcoming confidence;
2. challenge / direct gesture or crossed-arms confidence;
3. thinking / analytical chin-hand pose;
4. presenting / directional or open-hand gesture;
5. focused / serious game-face;
6. positive-result / restrained or high-energy celebration.

Any candidate with extra limbs, merged fingers, duplicate hands, face drift, broken wrist geometry or impossible overlap is rejected regardless of aesthetic quality.

## Character-to-live-object contact rule

Visual contact is permitted only when the contacted surface is geometrically stable and non-authoritative.

- Wheel: a character may point to or visually meet a fixed outer rim. The selectable/spinning wheel remains DOM/SVG authority.
- Club pack: a hand may visually meet a stable exterior rail/frame/pedestal. Do not couple raster anatomy to a moving reveal door or live result face.
- Trophy: character art may flank/present a trophy if it does not obscure the data surface or become the trophy-count authority.
- All character/source-art layers: `pointer-events:none`, no focus, no hidden input/state authority, removable without breaking comprehension.

If stable contact cannot survive the target responsive/state matrix, use an air gap.

## Overlap and composition rejection rules

Reject or redesign any composition where:

- hands, faces or hair cover critical labels, controls, inputs or data;
- foreground cards cut through anatomy in an unintended way;
- trophy/wheel/pack art blocks actionable controls;
- a decorative element becomes necessary to understand state;
- mobile merely scales the wide composition and produces unreadable controls;
- a reference-created feature cannot be reconciled to current product behavior;
- branded/external marks are treated as final assets without authority.
