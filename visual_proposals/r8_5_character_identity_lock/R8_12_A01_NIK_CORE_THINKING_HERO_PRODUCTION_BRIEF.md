## 2. Core identity rule

Preserve the same approved AI Nik, not a newly interpreted handsome male and not a photoreal reconstruction from the photographs.

Preserve especially:

- approved face silhouette and overall proportions
- jaw and chin structure
- eye spacing and eye shape
- brow density, shape and placement
- nose bridge, width and tip relationship
- relaxed mouth proportions
- hairline and characteristic dark hair mass
- beard and moustache distribution
- apparent age range
- the recognizable visual identity visible in the approved right-side Nik references and Reference C

Expression may move these features naturally. Identity may not redesign them.

## 3. Art direction

The shipping asset must feel like the approved Career Mode Showdown 17 AI character art: polished cinematic game-promo illustration with realistic anatomy, controlled stylization and authored digital-art finish.

It must NOT read as:

- a raw photograph
- a face-swap photograph
- hyperreal skin photography
- a generic stock-model portrait
- anime
- caricature
- plastic 3D avatar
- a different person inspired by Nik

Reference C is especially useful because it already demonstrates the desired balance between facial resemblance and stylized AI-character rendering. Do not make the isolation pass more photoreal than the source.

## 4. Pose and body extent

Primary reusable pose: calm thinking / tactical confidence.

Reference C already supplies the preferred pose family and should be preserved rather than re-invented when possible.

- near-front or subtle three-quarter orientation
- head mostly upright
- one hand naturally interacting with the chin/jaw area in the established approved direction
- other arm folded in a compositionally clean way
- torso visible to at least upper thigh if source extent permits; minimum acceptable extent is clean three-quarter upper body
- shoulders and elbows must not be cropped tightly by the final isolated canvas
- keep generous transparent padding around head, hand, shoulders and torso so CSS can crop the master differently per screen
- pose must remain believable when placed on the right side of desktop compositions
- do not create a reaching gesture that intrudes far toward center controls

## 5. Expression and gaze

Target expression: calm, thoughtful, self-assured, competitive but not aggressive.

- restrained closed-mouth or extremely subtle smile
- eyes alert and natural
- gaze approximately toward viewer or slightly inward toward central product content
- brows engaged but not exaggerated
- chin-hand contact should look anatomically natural
- no broad advertising smile
- no surprise, anger, sadness or theatrical squint

The intended read is: intelligent manager assessing the rivalry.

## 6. Wardrobe

Preferred family:

- black or charcoal tailored suit jacket
- black or very dark shirt
- clean modern manager styling
- minimal accessories
- the dark understated wristwatch visible in Reference C is acceptable, but it must not become an identity-critical detail

Do not embed team crests, official football marks, sponsor marks, FIFA branding, text, crown graphics or the number 17 into the clothing. Branding belongs to the website presentation layer, not the reusable character master.

## 7. Background and lighting

Required production goal: transparent background if technically clean.

Reference C contains stadium/environment and black outer padding. Those are reference context only and should be removed from A01.

If true transparency cannot be rendered cleanly, use a simple removable neutral/dark studio background with strong subject separation. No stadium, crowd, UI, typography, trophy, wheel, pack, menu cards or environmental storytelling in the final A01 master.

Lighting:

- preserve the flattering cinematic face lighting from Reference C where possible
- subtle warm/gold edge light is acceptable
- face must remain evenly readable
- avoid colored lighting that permanently binds the master to one screen
- no extreme bloom around hair or fingers that makes masking difficult

## 9. Forbidden drift

Reject the candidate immediately if any of the following happens:

- face reads as a different person from the approved Nik references
- isolation/editing repaints Reference C into a different face
- generation copies a literal supplied real photograph into the output
- art becomes raw photoreal instead of approved AI-character style
- jaw, nose, eye spacing, brows, hairline or beard pattern materially drift
- apparent age shifts materially
- hand/chin anatomy is broken or fused
- fingers cover important facial landmarks unnaturally
- wardrobe contains unrequested logos or football branding
- source canvas tightly crops the head, hand, elbows or shoulders
- background contains webpage/UI content
- a second person appears
- Daniel appears
- text appears

## 10. Production method

Preferred method is reference-preserving editing/isolation, not fresh face generation.

1. Start from the exact Reference C AI-character image.
2. Preserve the Nik face, hair, beard, expression, chin-hand pose, suit and overall body geometry as strongly as possible.
3. Remove the stadium/environment and black outer padding.
4. Produce a transparent or easily maskable isolated character with generous canvas padding.
5. Repair only edge/masking defects that are necessary for a clean asset.
6. Do not intentionally repaint or redesign the face merely to make the output look newly generated.
7. Only if clean isolation proves technically impossible may a new one-character generation be attempted, still using Reference C and the Tier 0 references as authority.

Do not produce a multi-expression board. Do not produce multiple lighting variants in one canvas. Do not generate Nik and Daniel together. Do not generate a webpage. Quality and identity consistency take precedence over novelty.
