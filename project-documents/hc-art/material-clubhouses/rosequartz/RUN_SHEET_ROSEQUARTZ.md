### Lessons from the pilot (built into batch 1)
1. Big plain rose quartz reads as plaster or smoke unless it has joints and the "hand-carved rose quartz bowl seen by candlelight" line (velvet pilot, Board T1/E1 6.5-7.5).
2. Astra warms rose and plum by 15-35 degrees: velvet #7A4474 lands at hue 337-345, stone #BD7CA6 at 343-350.
3. Tile words "authentic, traditional, hand-painted, imperfections" give an antique, chipped look; "brand new, flawless, freshly installed" is the fix (Persian tests).
4. Palace Board T1 came back evenly lit and too bright; a light-only edit ("only brightness moves; every hue stays") fixed it in one try. Batch 1 has the step-by-step falloff from the first try.
5. Palace Home T1 grew thin white branching lines in the stone and a pressed pattern in the velvet. Batch 1 says "misty milky clouds ... soft-edged like smoke", "no lines of any kind", "no pressed or embossed pattern".
6. A desk is not a counter: "a row of blocks exactly like the counter" turned the Office desk top into a block front (Office T1). Desks get "two big slabs lying flat, seen from above".
7. Lamp-lit rose quartz drifts peach (Office T1 lit desk hue 15); the pools line now says the lit stone keeps its mauve lean, never peach.
8. Words never used: marshmallow, marble, veins, glow (except "no glow halo"), glossy, mirror, polished, damask.
