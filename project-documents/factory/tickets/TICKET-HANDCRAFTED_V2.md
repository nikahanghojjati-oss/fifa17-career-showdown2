## The answer: rich but real

**What V1 got wrong.** V1 took out the AI tells (slogans, script name tags, crowns, gold light strips, mirror floors, sparkle crowds, glows) by emptying the rooms: bare stone and one lamp. You called that a downgrade, and it is. The old plates felt alive because their rooms were full; they looked fake because of the tells. V2 brings the life back with real things and keeps the tells out. It is still One House: one clubhouse above one football ground, on one night, and every screen is a room in it: the lounge (Home), the tunnel (Connect Players, Standings, Rivalry), the directors' box (League, Club), the office (Transfer War), the board room (Season Results, Final Winner, Career Statistics) and the archive (Trophy Room, Legacy).

**Dress each room like a real set.** A room photographed by an interior photographer looks rich because of the building and a few things people really left there, never because of gold or glow. Ten rules:
1. **Three distances.** Front: the counter. Middle: the men and the furniture beside them. Back: a wall 4-15 m away, or the ground through the glass. The building gives most of the richness (depth, oak, stone columns, cases, a live ground), then big furniture, then a few small things.
2. **One hero, and a reason for everything.** Each room has one thing a visitor names first, and every object answers "who left this here tonight?": a drink at kick-off, a coat from the cold stand, the scouting files, the first ball.
3. **Count the things.** Desktop MEDIUM: 2-4 named things. RICH: up to 6. Lamps count; walls, the rug and the view do not. Phone: 2 things and 1 lamp. Never more than 6 in one prompt, or 3 on one surface.
4. **Beside, behind or below the men, never in front.** Nothing stands in front of a man above the counter lip. Lit things stay at least 6% of the width from any hair, dark things 3% from any outline. Only walls, doors, columns and window frames may pass behind a man.
5. **Calm zones stay real and quiet.** The title band, the panel areas, the empty centre column and everything under the lip are plain oak, night glass or the counter front: a real surface with nothing on it and nothing bright. One exception, Home: its heading (x 2-31, y 68-82) sits over the lounge floor, so there only dark leather, the rug and the floor show, with no pale wear, glass or brass.
6. **Gold is trim.** Dull brass only on the lip, window frames, lamp metal, handles, hooks and the ball stand, darker in the corners. Apart from the lip and the frames nothing gold is bigger than a hand, and there are no gold objects: the HC 06 cup is the only one. No brass trim on pillars (it reads as the old light strips). Warmth comes from oak, leather, ochre wool and lamplight.
7. **Lamps a person would place** (the family below). At most 2 lamps in view on desktop and 1 on the phone, never a matching pair, always dimmer than the faces, nothing on the ceiling.
8. **Blank by nature.** Things with no print in real life: leather balls, plain glass, porcelain, wool, oak, leather. Books lie flat with the page edges to the camera, papers face down, bottles bare, and every new thing gets "nothing written on it".
9. **Used, not staged.** Wear on 2-3 things per room, one mark each: arms rubbed paler, a water ring, a bent corner. No matching pairs: Daniel's side and Nik's side never mirror. Every object stands on something, with a small soft contact shadow.
10. **Rich enough, measured.** Claude measures every desktop on the 1X crop with the men masked (table below). The board room and the box may be up to 10 points flatter, with 6 points fewer mid-tones, because their calm zones are big. Every bright spot must be a named lamp, a lit case, a roof lamp bank or the lit pitch, and the 1X WebP stays at 200 KB or less at quality 75. The numbers are Claude's alarm: a room goes back only if Claude can name the missing layer.

**The lamp family: nothing hangs.** Every lamp is warm 2700 K, dull dark brass, bulb hidden, and makes one soft pool on a wall, a table or a case, never on a man; a shade shows its pleats, with no bright ring. Every ceiling is plain, with no fixtures. The light on your faces stays as it is: Claude's lock puts it back, and every prompt says only that it comes from in front of you, from behind the camera. Rich adds things, not light.
- **The four lamps:** the **wall lamp** (brass arm, pleated cream linen shade, HC 01's lamp) on the lounge pier (right) and the board room's left wall; the **table or desk lamp** (the same shade on a turned dark-oak or brass base) on the lounge sideboard (left) and the office desk (right); the **case lamp**, hidden in the top of a glass case or cabinet, in the box drinks cabinet (right) and the two archive cases (the right one dimmer); the **caged bulkhead** (oval ribbed glass in a wire cage, cast brass) on the tunnel's right wall only.

*Real rooms behind this:* Highbury's board room was "panelled in varnished oak" (Arsenal.com, undated, page © 2025); Aston Villa's Directors Club has brass skirting, dark oak floors and low panelling (DesignCurial, 4 Oct 2017); Tottenham's Tunnel Club is "a rich, subdued interior" (Design Week, undated); stylists switch every lamp on and pull one chair out (Houzz, 6 Apr 2011); one hero plus two smaller pieces, at most five per surface (Homes & Gardens, 25 Apr 2025).

## How to prompt ChatGPT image for detail

1. **One order every time, and repeat the keep list:** keep the men → the job of each picture → what changes → where things go → materials → light → finish → Remove and Never add. (RunDiffusion, *ChatGPT Image 2.5 Prompt Guide*, 2026-10-07, updated 2026-10-21; fal, *GPT Image 2 Prompting Guide*, 2026-04-21; OpenAI Cookbook image prompting guide, 2026-04-21.)
2. **Pin each thing to a surface, a side and a % box, and say where it must not go;** always add "nothing stands in front of either man". (RunDiffusion, 2026-10-21; fal, 2026-04-21.)
3. **Give an exact count, most important first, then forbid extras.** At most about 6 named things per prompt: our own estimate, since no source gives a number. Without a count ChatGPT fills empty walls, and those walls are our calm zones. (RunDiffusion, 2026-10-21: "Do not add or duplicate furniture"; fal, 2026-04-21: "one revision per turn"; eWeek, *ChatGPT Image Editing Mistakes*, 2026-05-15: many edits in one prompt give "messy results"; Rewarx, undated, weak.)
4. **Name materials and finishes, never moods.** Say "a real photograph" and how it was taken, never "photorealistic" alone. (fal, 2026-04-21: "Mood language buries the brief"; eWeek, 2026-05-15: "Make it photorealistic" gives overly smooth surfaces and artificial highlights; OpenAI Cookbook, 2026-04-21; TechRadar, 2026-04-28: a "more cinematic and dramatic" prompt produced exactly that.)
5. **Describe the blank surface, then one short no-text line;** best of all, pick things that are blank in real life. (eWeek, 2026-05-15; fal, 2026-04-21: "No text. No watermark.")
6. **Never name what you do not want; give the light a real, low fixture.** Write "plain ceiling with no fixtures" and name the wall, table or case lamp. (From practice: V1 HC 04 try B, 8 Nov 2026, where the pendant sentence drew pendants.)
7. **Say where the light comes from, give every object a contact shadow, and put wear on single objects.** Global wear words make grain, which adds noise and file weight. (RunDiffusion, 2026-10-21: "reflections and contact shadows", "subtle stone variation"; fal, 2026-04-21: "chipped paint", "worn canvas"; eWeek, 2026-06-15: light direction and shadows mattered most in background swaps.)
8. **A keep list does not lock the geometry.** Label each picture by its job and compare options from the same original (both Home levels start from one file); Claude measures the lip and every calm-zone box after every run. (RunDiffusion, 2026-10-21: "Preservation instructions do not lock geometry"; fal, 2026-04-21: "Labeling each input by role…")

- **Words never in a prompt, not even in a ban** (the word itself calls the object up): pendant, chandelier, ceiling lamp, downlight, spotlight, recessed light, hang, hung, hanging. Write "on a hook", "over the arm", "fixed to the wall".
- **Words never used to describe** (Remove and Never add lines may still name banned things): luxury, luxurious, premium, opulent, lavish, high-end, VIP, elite, royal, regal, majestic, iconic; stunning, gorgeous, beautiful, breathtaking, epic, dramatic, cinematic, masterpiece, award-winning, hyper-detailed, ultra-realistic, "photorealistic" alone; 8K, 4K, HDR, octane, Unreal, render, CGI; magazine, editorial, Architectural Digest, cover, poster style, brand; glowing, shimmering, sparkling, gleaming, glossy, polished, golden, neon, beam, rays, atmosphere; weathered, aged, distressed, grungy, vintage, film grain; champions, legend, and trophy outside HC 06. The level names MEDIUM and RICH never appear in pasted text.
- **Left out on purpose, in every room:** flowers and paintings (where ChatGPT adds gloss, signatures or faces); colour comes from ochre-and-black wool instead.
